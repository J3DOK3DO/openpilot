# Findings and limits

## A. FDCAN2, rate, and topology

**Observed:** The first route has 2,603 `controlsMismatch` event-message occurrences and 10,873 of 10,912 sampled Panda states with active `interruptRateCan2`. Route 1 has 49 and 230/230; neither route enabled openpilot. Route 2 has neither active fault nor `controlsMismatch` and did enable with stock ACC. The first route's maximum `can1.irq0CallRate` is 17,862/s; later peaks are 9,700 and 14,139/s. The first-route Pandad `0x00000008` maps to `FAULT_INTERRUPT_RATE_CAN_2` (`panda/board/sys/sys.h:34`; C6-12 `panda/board/faults_declarations.h:11`). `faultTemp` is sticky and cannot by itself establish an active fault. These are observations of Dom 7 only, not measurements on C6-12.

**Mapping:** `panda/board/drivers/fdcan.h:5,70,267-279` maps zero-based `can1` health to physical `FDCAN2`, with IT0 handling RX and errors, IT1 TX. Both trees register that IRQ with `CAN_INTERRUPT_RATE` and the CAN_2 fault (C6-12 `panda/board/drivers/fdcan.h:245-257`). The H7 limit is 16,000/s in current `panda/board/stm32h7/stm32h7_config.h:16-28` and C6-12 lines 15-26. Both `interrupts.h` implementations increment per-vector calls, fault above the registered maximum, and publish one-second call rates (current lines 59-76, 91-114; C6-12 lines 15-34, 47-59). Current added timer/NVIC checks do not change this over-rate decision. The 17,862/s measurement exceeds the invariant limit by 1,862/s (11.64%). This is enough to explain the active Panda interrupt-rate fault, which is a plausible direct reason for controls mismatch and no engagement. It does not identify what caused the IRQ excess.

**Possible contributors:** IT0 includes RX-new-message and protocol-error interrupts (`panda/board/stm32h7/llfdcan.h:209-219`), while its handler drains FIFO0 and handles error flags (`panda/board/drivers/fdcan.h:157-175,258-262`). Route 0 `can1.receiveErrorCnt` reaches 127 and `totalErrorCnt` 35,235; route 1 total errors reach 70,778. Route 2 also has error counts (35,438 peak) without active rate fault. The error counters are cumulative and not causal proof. High traffic, repeated protocol errors, line/wiring/transceiver conditions, bus speed mismatch, and OBD multiplex transition are hypotheses. The zero-based bus defaults are 500 kbit/s nominal and 2 Mbit/s data (`panda/board/drivers/can_common.h:144-146`); actual per-route configured speed, CAN-FD frames, bit timing and electrical state were not established. The H7 FDCAN clock is PLL1Q at 80 MHz (`panda/board/stm32h7/clock.h:5,112-113`; bitrate prescaler `llfdcan_declarations.h:7`). ELM327 param 0 selects OBD CAN2 versus normal mode in `panda/board/main.c:72-84`; Red board GPIO and transceiver routing depends on harness flip in `panda/board/boards/red.h:28-63`. The actual board revision, harness orientation and transition timing are unverified. C6-12 has no evidence-backed lower rate or different watchdog. Its `fdcan.h` forwarding and current MPU wrapping differ; timing impact is unknown without comparable measurement.

## B. Honda Bosch-C profile and AOL

Recorded CarParams on all three routes: `HONDA_ACCORD_11G`, Honda Bosch, safety word 80 = CANFD 16 + MVL 64, flags 2180 = Bosch 4 + CANFD 128 + Hybrid 2048, `pcmCruise=true`, openpilot longitudinal false, alternative experience 0. Current flag definitions: `opendbc_repo/opendbc/car/honda/values.py:85-123`; Accord platform lines 174-180, 218-224. Interface sets Bosch safety, denies alpha longitudinal for CANFD, uses PCM cruise, and adds 16/64 (`interface.py:40-58,232-241`). CarState derives cruise enabled from `POWERTRAIN_DATA.ACC_STATUS`, available from `MAIN_ON`, and maps the physical LKAS setting to button events (`carstate.py:73-76,209-210,234-237`). Camera LKAS HUD is parsed at lines 219-226. The route 2 button count contains 39 LKAS press/release pairs; that is physical input evidence, not independent lateral authorization.

Current stock AOL eligibility is exact: `opendbc_repo/opendbc/car/honda/stock_aol.py:52-84` dispatches Accord to `accord_mvl_stock.py:6-14`, which requires the unmodified stock safety profile and permitted flags/transmission. `openpilot/starpilot/car/honda/aol.py:37-52` adds alternative experience 32 only when policy/intent qualifies; `openpilot/selfdrive/car/card.py:373-401` gates settings, intent and marked profile. Marked native acceptance uses exact model/word (`openpilot/starpilot/car/honda/aol.py:62-80`), and the native observation requires requested and allowed lateral (`opendbc_repo/opendbc/car/honda/stock_aol.py:127-139`). The native request also depends on valid fresh car state, session, inventory and matching safety receipt (`openpilot/starpilot/car/honda/aol.py:108-132`). Saved `AlwaysOnLateral=1` is a preference. The recorded alternative experience 0 means the marked profile 32 was not present in the sampled CarParams; it does not prove which gate failed. Route 2 lateral-active with stock ACC is ordinary coupled behavior, not proof of independent AOL. No eligibility or authorization bypass follows from the preference.

C6-12 has Accord platform and CANFD safety word construction (`opendbc_repo/opendbc/car/honda/values.py:197`; `interface.py:156-163,324-333`), SCM/LKAS parsing (`carstate.py:105-112,275-289`), and a generic Honda AOL availability check (`starpilot/common/starpilot_variables.py:347-348`) that feeds the preference in `selfdrive/car/card.py:165-169,242-246`. It lacks the current `honda/stock_aol.py` and `accord_mvl_stock.py` marked native path. That is a source-level feature difference, not evidence that C6-12 would have engaged independently or avoided the Panda fault. C6-12 includes Accord radar/tick replacement and control tests; their vehicle behavior is outside this log campaign.

## C. Other health signals

Route 2 has one `commIssue` involving `carOutput`. Current card publishes `carOutput` (`openpilot/selfdrive/car/card.py:198,1033-1044`) and selfdrived monitors it (`openpilot/selfdrive/selfdrived/selfdrived.py:196`); generic all-check failure adds `commIssue` and logs invalid, not-alive, frequency status (lines 644-673). One event does not prove a persistent producer failure; per-service timing and validity around that event remain unknown. UI FPS warnings originate from a 60 FPS target and periodic threshold check (`openpilot/system/ui/lib/application.py:24-30,919-941`); dropped UI frames do not establish camera/model frame loss. u-blox NACK and almanac storage errors have their own logging paths (`openpilot/system/ubloxd/pigeond.py:96-112,154-161,334-337`); their causal link to CAN2 or control engagement is unproven. The summary's empty manager failure map is not proof that no transient process issue occurred. No raw log text is reproduced here because it can include identifiers.

## Conclusion and next diagnostic scope

The supported direct mechanism for routes 0/1 is the active Panda FDCAN2 interrupt-rate fault with controls mismatch; source and telemetry do not distinguish traffic from repeated error interrupts or a physical/configuration problem. Route 2 shows the same stock profile functioning without active Panda faults. Next offline work could correlate second-by-second IT0, error-counter deltas, bus speed, Panda safety transition, and harness metadata from existing logs; compare with a C6-12 log only if one exists. None of this qualifies driving or changes an authorization gate.
